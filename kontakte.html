Who Helps — and With What

Reordered: not by discipline but by function for the search — what a person concretely contributes when you talk to them about noise and possible new signals. "The search" here does not mean lifting hidden ore, but shifting the border of the imaginable: conceiving models in which the discarded counts. Each role marks one first contact; below it, who brings something similar, so you don't write to three people for the same angle.

Five roles · what to expect from them
①
Find the signal — actually measure/compute where a new signal hides."Where in the discarded is a real signal, and how would one reach it?"
②
Aggregate & value the signal — turn many noisy sources into a robust, possibly viable signal."How do you extract it cleanly — and what is it worth?"
③
Separate signal from artifact — the review instance against pseudo-signals."Is this real, or did you fool yourself?"
④
Understand when a pattern is a signal — the philosophy-of-science grounding."What does it even mean that a signal 'arises' here?"
⑤
Concept, discourse & narrative — the media/cultural-theory framing."How do you understand and tell this — and where does the story tip over?"
★ first contact the most obvious entry to this rolealt. brings something similar — not needed on top
①

Find the signal

Neuro · BCI · sensory & collective dynamics

Benjamin Lindner ★ first contact

HU Berlin & Bernstein Center (BCCN) · "Theory of Complex Systems and Neurophysics" · stochastic resonance, signal transmission in noisy neurons
branch 02 · 08
Contribution
The theoretical core of the question when neural variability carries information rather than disturbing it. A feasibility check for any "signal hides in the noise" idea; access to the BCCN environment.
Caveat
Fundamental physics, not product thinking — asks "is the effect real?", not "is it marketable?". As an early reality check, a virtue.

Antonenko Lab & R. Fleischmann

Uni Greifswald / Charité · aperiodic EEG as an age/state marker, norms across the lifespan
branch 08 · 09
Contribution
The most concrete group for the aperiodic EEG part as a repeatable endpoint — the most mature "noise-becomes-marker" case (setup 03). Clinically grounded, with real data.
Caveat
Clinical context; the "rejuvenation" reading from the tech scene goes beyond the norm studies.

Surjo Soekadar

Charité Berlin · non-invasive brain-computer interfaces, real-time interpretation of brain signals
branch 08
Contribution
Works at the edge of what signal can be gained from noisy brain activity in real time. An ideal reality check: "what can today's BCIs really do vs. media hype."
Caveat
Clinical-technical focus; future speculation is not his terrain — which tests the ground under the ideas.

Petra Ritter

Charité / BIH · brain simulation, "The Virtual Brain" · linking measured data and models
branch 08 · 04
Contribution
The transition measurement → model → simulation: how do you test a signal hypothesis in the model before collecting it from people?
Caveat
Prominent institute lead — more reachable via the working group.
further in this role · compact
Jan Benda Uni Tübingen · neuroethology
Neural coding & signal separation in sensory systems (weakly electric fish): how a nervous system pulls relevant signals from competing stimuli. Cooperates with the Berlin HU group. A concrete signal-practice contact beside Lindner.
Tönjes · Zaks HU Berlin · physics
Noise, heterogeneity & synchronization in coupled systems: how collective patterns emerge from noisy individual processes. The dynamic side of "the network perceives more"; often easier to reach as working contacts.
Ramsey (NL) UMC Utrecht · BCI
Internationally leading in implanted BCIs for locked-in patients (signal from intracranial EEG). The international complement to Soekadar if invasive methods are in play.
②

Aggregate & value the signal

Signal extraction · aggregation · economics

HU/DIW econometrics ★ first contact

HU Berlin & DIW · high-dimensional time series (formerly IRTG 1792), Härdle, Schienle · microstructure noise, prediction markets
branch 05
Contribution
The mathematics of signal extraction from noisy, high-dimensional data — and the economic question of what the signal is worth. Where "the network perceives more" is worked through numerically.
Caveat
Thinks in financial-market categories; transfer to biological data isn't automatic. The structure of the argument, less the content of the signal.
This role is thinly staffed — deliberately. The aggregation math is highly specialized; one strong first contact suffices at first. If your approach targets crowd/sensor aggregation specifically, the MIT CCI environment (prediction markets) joins internationally — see Global.
③

Separate signal from artifact

the review instance against pseudo-signals

QUEST Center ★ first contact

Berlin Institute of Health @ Charité · Center for Responsible Research · replicability, p-hacking, "is this just noise?"
branch 04
Contribution
Tests whether a found "signal" survives replication — before time and money flow in. With many features & few subjects, the decisive filter against pseudo-clocks.
Caveat
The real risk is skipping this role. The only station whose job is "no, that's noise" — drop it and the GCP-like constellation remains.

Explainable-AI group

Fraunhofer HHI / TU Berlin / BIFOLD · Samek, Lapuschkin, Müller · what did a model really learn?
branch 04
Contribution
The same question as QUEST — real or artifact? — but for AI models: which correlations a model uses, when it reaches a result via irrelevant features ("Clever Hans"). For small conversations, Lapuschkin is often more realistic.
Caveat
AI focus, no neuro measurement path. Overlaps with QUEST: both test spurious signals — QUEST empirically-statistically, XAI model-internally. Both needed only if AI models are in play.
④

Understand when a pattern is a signal

Philosophy of science · data epistemology

Uljana Feest ★ first contact

Leibniz Universität Hannover · epistemology of experiment, "stabilization of phenomena," cognitive science
branch 04 · 06
Contribution
Distinguishes patterns in the data from hidden regularities and works on how a stable phenomenon emerges from uncertain data. Philosophy of science gets no closer to your core question; with reference to psychology/neuro and replication.
Caveat
Supplies the criteria, not the apparatus — which is exactly why she's valuable beside the practice contacts. Active, easy to reach.

Hans-Jörg Rheinberger

MPI for the History of Science, Berlin (emeritus) · "epistemic things" / experimental systems
branch 06
Contribution
His "epistemic thing" — the resistant that only becomes an object of knowledge — is almost a definition of "noise becomes signal"; brings in Derrida's "différance" (your register).
Caveat
Emeritus & very well known — a reference figure, not a regular working contact. Overlaps with Feest: both "how a phenomenon emerges/stabilizes"; Feest more practical & reachable, Rheinberger deeper & more theoretical.

Sabina Leonelli

TU Munich / University of Exeter · philosophy of data-intensive science · "Data Journeys"
branch 04
Contribution
What happens to data when it is cleaned, standardized, reused — and what is lost in the process. Hits your bottleneck ("the raw signal gets filtered away") theoretically.
Caveat
Overlaps with the data-epistemology corner (Gitelman/Bowker below): all treat "data is never raw." Leonelli is the most philosophically systematic; the others media-historical.
data-is-never-raw · the media-historical variant
Lisa Gitelman NYU
Editor of "Raw Data Is an Oxymoron" — the title is your theme: data is no raw material but culturally "cooked." The media-historical version of Leonelli's point.
Geoffrey Bowker UC Irvine
With S. L. Star, "Sorting Things Out": classifying and the thereby discarded / made invisible. (Star herself deceased — work/source.)
⑤

Concept, discourse & narrative

Media & cultural theory · future discourse

Bernhard Siegert ★ first contact

Bauhaus-Universität Weimar · IKKM · cultural-techniques research, "representation and noise" · Kittler's successor
branch 06
Contribution
The media-archaeological, material version: signal/noise as a media-historical category, Foucault/Deleuze-inflected. A bridge from French theory to German media studies.
Caveat
Concept & interpretation, no measurement path. Don't confuse the two registers (understanding vs. measuring).

Orit Halpern

TU Dresden · cybernetics, digital infrastructures, future narratives
branch 06
Contribution
How models, capital and future narratives connect — exactly the critical perspective on your monetization / "tech-bubble" strand: how a signal idea becomes a narrative with capital.
Caveat
Discourse analysis, not signal practice. Unique in this role — little overlap with the others.
further in this role · compact
Miyazaki · Schürmer HU Berlin / Halle
Younger German-language voices: "algorhythmics" (Miyazaki) and noise/glitch as an epistemic-aesthetic phenomenon (Schürmer). Closer to digital present-day practice than the canonical theory. Overlaps with Siegert — similar register, younger generation.
N. Katherine Hayles USA · literature/media
Information, noise & embodiment (How We Became Posthuman, 2025 Bacteria to AI). The literary/posthumanist version of the information concept; international, as a reference.
MPIWG (Tkaczyk circle) Berlin
History of knowledge of "background noise" — the narrative that carries the long line "noise becomes signal." Overlaps with Rheinberger & Siegert on the historical axis.
// where people bring you the same thing · what you can spare
Feest ↔ Rheinberger
Both: "how a phenomenon emerges/stabilizes from uncertain data." Feest suffices for the start (practical, reachable); Rheinberger when it should go deeper & more theoretical.
Leonelli ↔ Gitelman/Bowker
All: "data is never raw, cleaning throws something away." Leonelli covers it systematically; Gitelman/Bowker only additionally if interested in the media-historical perspective.
QUEST ↔ Explainable-AI
Both test spurious signals. QUEST for empirical findings, XAI for AI models — both needed only if your method actually uses ML.
Siegert ↔ Miyazaki/Schürmer ↔ MPIWG
All: media/knowledge-historical framing of signal/noise. One suffices for the framing — Siegert most established, Miyazaki/Schürmer closer to the present, MPIWG most historical.
Soekadar ↔ Ramsey
Both BCI / signal from brain activity. Soekadar (Berlin, non-invasive) as first contact; Ramsey (Utrecht) only if interested in invasive implants.
Lindner ↔ Tönjes/Zaks ↔ Benda
All: noise/dynamics in excitable or coupled systems. Lindner as the theoretical first contact; Tönjes/Zaks for collective dynamics, Benda for sensory signal separation — depending on direction.

// how to use this page

The five roles map the path of the search: ① find a signal, ② aggregate & value it, ③ test whether it's real, ④ understand when a pattern is a signal, ⑤ grasp & tell it. For a first pass the five ★ first contacts suffice — one per role. They cover the whole arc without redundancy.

The compact collector entries and the overlap table are for later: they show whom to add deliberately when your approach sharpens in one direction (invasive BCIs → Ramsey; sensory signal separation → Benda; media-historical data critique → Gitelman/Bowker) — and whom you can spare because someone else covers the same.
