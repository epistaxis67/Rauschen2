An Epistemology of Noise · 2018

Cécile Malaspina · foreword Ray Brassier · Bloomsbury
The philosophical sum of the topic and the one title linking both worlds: disputes that noise is mere error/disorder, and treats "noise trading" explicitly. A bridge from theory to usable signal.

Noise Matters: Towards an Ontology of Noise · 2013

Greg Hainge · Bloomsbury
Noise not as mere sound but as an ontological property of all relations. The philosophically most ambitious version in the sound-studies field; covers film, music, aesthetics.
Publisher ↗purchase

The Parasite · 1980

Michel Serres · Fr. Le parasite · Univ. of Minnesota Press
The primal scene: "parasite" means both freeloader and interfering noise. Noise as the constitutive third that both disturbs and enables communication. "The city makes the noise, but the noise makes the city."
Univ. of Minnesota Press · died 2019

// how this collection relates to the search

The entries are deliberately not sorted by theory school but by medium and by what helps on the way to signal: A supplies the mechanism, B sharpens the method, C gives concept and language. Notable is the field's split — the statistical-predictive line (Silver, Kahneman, stochastic resonance) carries the "how do you find signal," the cultural/media-theoretical (Serres, Malaspina, Hainge) the "what does it mean and how do you tell it." And in the spirit of the manifesto's guiding thesis, the group-C texts especially help grasp the discarded not as raw material but as the border of the imaginable.

For the actual aim — drawing people who talk about noise into the search — the ▸ reachable-marked authors are the anchor points: Malaspina, Hainge, Rheinberger, Leonelli, Feest, Gitelman, Bowker, Hayles, Kahn, Hegarty, Abbott, Ward, Mills. Many of them also appear in the contact cards — literature and contacts are deliberately interlocked: where a work convinces, the person is usually also a contact. The podcasts are the most practical channel, because they continuously gather exactly such voices — several of the linked episodes (Hayles, Leonelli, Rheinberger) are direct audio samples of people from the contact cards.
