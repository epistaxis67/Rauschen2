Global — Noise to Signal

People and institutions worldwide who, from the extended notion of noise, could help turn interference/data trash into signal — ordered by degree of speculativeness: from established and documented to approaches at or beyond the scientific fringe. The rating is my assessment, not a judgment of the people; it should ease your filtering, not replace it. A sixth band at the end runs crosswise: the cultural/theory side, marked by proximity to post-structuralism.

// established · solid foundation

Dennis Lo · Centre for Novostics

Chinese University of Hong Kong · fragmentomics, liquid biopsy · pioneer of cell-free DNA
branch 02 · established
Noise→signal
Turned what counted as cellular waste in the blood (DNA fragments of dead cells) into a diagnostic signal: fragment length, break points and end motifs betray cell origin and disease.
Useful for
The living proof that "data trash becomes signal" works, really and at billion-dollar scale. A model for the figure of thought; a reference for anything drawing information from discarded material.
Rating
Fully established, in clinical use, repeatedly awarded. No speculation risk — rather the seal of quality for the whole idea.

Iain Couzin · MPI of Animal Behavior

MPI Konstanz / Uni Konstanz · collective behaviour, emergent sensing · author of the fish-swarm study
branch 05 · established
Noise→signal
Showed experimentally that a group perceives stimuli that lie below the individual's threshold — exactly your "several perceive a signal in the noise." Uses VR for animal experiments.
Useful for
The most theoretically and experimentally grounded address for collective perception. If your approach rests on "the network sees more," this is the source of the mechanism.
Rating
Highly established (heavily cited, MPI director). The mechanism is documented; only the transfer from animal groups to human networks is speculative — that's where your work begins.

Stochastic resonance school

internationally distributed · F. Moss † (shaped the field), L. Ward (UBC), Chapeau-Blondeau (Angers), D. Abbott (Adelaide), T. Geisel (MPI)
branch 02 · 08 · established
Noise→signal
The physical-mathematical foundation for how noise can lift a weak signal over the perception threshold (stochastic resonance). Applied from neurons to mass spectrometry.
Useful for
The theory behind "added noise makes the hidden visible" — directly relevant to setup 01 & sensory methods. Chapeau-Blondeau/Abbott for the detection math.
Rating
An established field since the 1980s. Solid; the line to speculation runs only at exaggerated application promises (e.g. therapeutic).
// emerging · young but viable

Apple ML Health · biosignal foundation models

Apple / Apple Heart and Movement Study · S. Abbaspourazad, I. Shapiro et al. · PPG/ECG/accelerometer at scale
branch 04 · 08 · emerging
Noise→signal
Trains large models on raw, "noisy" everyday biosignals from ~280,000 people — exactly the raw material usually filtered away, here as the training basis. Shows that the raw signal encodes demographics & health state.
Useful for
The industrial proof that scaling into the raw signal finds signal. Model/method are published — usable as a blueprint even without Apple's data access. A reference for the "raw data" bottleneck.
Rating
Methodically solid but young; corporate context (no open data access). "Emerging" because the clinical value of the found signals is still being validated.

MIT Center for Collective Intelligence

MIT · Thomas Malone et al. · a measurable "collective IQ" of groups
branch 05 · emerging
Noise→signal
Investigates whether groups have their own measurable "collective intelligence factor" — a signal at the level of the collective, emerging from the noisy individual contributions.
Useful for
The bridge from Couzin's animal swarms to human networks and their productization (team tools, forecast aggregation). Closest to your monetization question.
Rating
Reproducible, but the effect is modest and the replication picture mixed. "Emerging" — solid enough to build on, too weak for big promises.
// exploratory · promising, evidence thin

Voice-biomarker field

incl. NIH "Voice as a Biomarker of Health," Mayo Clinic, various startups · paralinguistics as a health signal
branch 08 · exploratory
Noise→signal
Reads from the how of speaking (jitter, prosody) — the trace irrelevant to transcription — markers for neurological, psychiatric and respiratory states.
Useful for
A direct link to setup 04. Large consortia are building open datasets — connectable, low hardware threshold.
Rating
Promising, but reproducibility and generalization are open; much hype in the startup scene. Privacy (voice = identifying) is central here.

EEG aging-clock scene

international, partly industry-adjacent · aperiodic EEG (FOOOF/Voytek method), various "brain age" groups & longevity startups
branch 08 · 09 · exploratory
Noise→signal
Turns the aperiodic 1/f part — for decades computed away as "baseline noise" — into a target for brain age and state.
Useful for
The core of setup 03; the internationally broadest activity at the neuro/longevity seam. The method (FOOOF) is openly available.
Rating
The marker itself is documented; the "rejuvenation" products around it are exploratory to oversold. Exactly here real signal parts from good storytelling — a review instance is needed.
// fringe · scientifically contested

Micro-expression / affective-computing fringe

various labs & vendors · emotion from micro facial movement, skin-color pulse (rPPG), "lie detection"
branch 07 · 08 · fringe
Noise→signal
Wants to read inner states reliably from micro-movements and skin-color flicker — otherwise image noise.
Useful for
Technically overlaps with setup 06 (rPPG is real). Interesting as a signal source if tightly bounded.
Rating
Emotion/lie detection from the face is scientifically strongly contested (weak construct validity). The physiological part (rPPG) is solid, the psychological reading is not. Separate them.
// speculative · not recognized · for demarcation

HeartMath / Global Coherence · GCP 2.0

HeartMath Institute; successor to the Global Consciousness Project · global HRV/magnetometer net, "collective consciousness"
branch 05 (distortion) · speculative
Noise→signal
Claims a global net of HRV and magnetic-field sensors picks up a collective-consciousness signal linking humans, the geomagnetic field and Schumann resonances.
Useful for
Above all as a warning example & contrast foil: it is exactly the seductive version of your idea — "the network feels something together" — only without tenable method. Shows how the narrative tips over.
Rating
Scientifically not recognized. Moving-target shifting, post-hoc event selection, no robust causality. Listed here deliberately so the border is visible — not as a cooperation recommendation.
// theory & cultural studies · own axis: proximity to post-structuralism
This band stands deliberately crosswise to the speculativeness scale above: here it is not "documented vs. undocumented" but the concept of noise — what it means, how it shifts, what epistemic function interference has. The dots on the right (■) show proximity to post-structuralist theory. Only living, active thinkers — the canonical works (Serres, Kittler, Barthes) belong in a bibliography, not a contact card.

Cécile Malaspina

Collège International de Philosophie, Paris · King's College London · author of An Epistemology of Noise (2018) · Simondon translator
branch 06 · core text
Noise→signal
The philosophical sum of the topic: a transdisciplinary epistemology of noise that re-evaluates Shannon/Weaver and disputes that noise is reducible to error/disorder. Explicitly treats "noise trading" too.
Useful for
Your central theoretical reference point — links information theory, natural science and critical theory. Via Simondon and the Brassier foreword, directly connectable to your preferred line of thought.
Post-str.
Very high — Simondon, Deleuze milieu, speculative realism (Brassier). Exactly your register.

Greg Hainge

University of Queensland · Professor of French · author of Noise Matters: Towards an Ontology of Noise (2013)
branch 06 · 07 · ontology
Noise→signal
Develops a Deleuzian ontology of noise: noise is not merely sound but names the "ontological impedance" of all relations — it is everywhere something enters into relation.
Useful for
If you want to grasp noise ontologically rather than only technically — the philosophically most ambitious version in the sound-studies field. Also covers film, music, aesthetics.
Post-str.
Very high — explicitly Deleuze/Guattari, continental philosophy.

Bernhard Siegert

Bauhaus-Universität Weimar · IKKM · cultural-techniques research · "representation and noise"
branch 06 · cultural technique
Noise→signal
A leading figure of German cultural-techniques research in Kittler's succession; works with the pair representation/noise as a media-historical category of analysis.
Useful for
The media-archaeological, material version of noise; a bridge between French theory (Foucault, Deleuze) and German media studies. Reachable institutionally via the IKKM Weimar.
Post-str.
High — Foucault/Deleuze-inflected, but with its own technical-materialist accent.

Hans-Jörg Rheinberger

MPI for the History of Science, Berlin (emeritus) · "epistemic things" / experimental systems · Toward a History of Epistemic Things (1997)
branch 06 · key figure
Noise→signal
His concept of the "epistemic thing" — the indeterminate, resistant ("resistance notice") that only gradually becomes an object of knowledge — is almost a definition of your "noise becomes signal." Asks explicitly how the new arises in empirical science.
Useful for
The theoretical heart of the conversation about the search: how an experimental system brings forth objects one does not yet know the identity of. Brings in Derrida's "différance" — exactly your register.
Post-str.
Very high — works explicitly with Derrida. Emeritus, more a reference figure than a working contact.

Uljana Feest

Leibniz Universität Hannover · epistemology of experiment, "stabilization of phenomena," psychology/cognitive science
branch 04 · 06 · key figure
Noise→signal
Her core concept hits your point almost verbatim: she distinguishes phenomena as patterns in the data (surface regularities) from hidden regularities — and asks when a pattern in uncertain data becomes a stable, real signal.
Useful for
Perhaps the most precise German-language interlocutor for "when is a pattern a signal" — works concretely on psychology/neuro. Links philosophy of science to the safeguarding question (replication).
Post-str.
Historical epistemology; analytically inflected but close to practice and phenomena. Active, easy to reach.

Sabina Leonelli

TU Munich / University of Exeter · philosophy of data-intensive science · Data Journeys in the Sciences
branch 04 · data epistemology
Noise→signal
Leading on what happens to data when it is collected, cleaned, standardized and reused — and what is lost in cleaning. Treats noise not as a filtering problem but as part of producing usable data.
Useful for
Hits your bottleneck from the devices card ("the raw signal gets filtered away") at the theoretical level. Ideal for thinking through data practice critically before building a signal on it.
Post-str.
More STS/philosophy of science than post-structuralism — but highly relevant in substance. Active.

Orit Halpern

TU Dresden · history of science/technology, cybernetics, digital infrastructures, future narratives
branch 06 · future discourse
Noise→signal
Works on how scientific models, capital and future narratives connect — how visions of the future become infrastructures and investment programs.
Useful for
Exactly the critically reflective perspective on your monetization / "tech-bubble" strand: helps understand how a signal idea becomes a narrative with capital — and what that does to the thing itself.
Post-str.
Critical theory / media theory of cybernetics. Active, in Germany (Dresden).

// how to read the scale

From top to bottom what rises is not the boldness of the idea but the risk that the "signal" is none. The established addresses (Lo, Couzin, stochastic resonance) have already documented the noise-becomes-signal figure — they show the basic idea holds. The emerging ones (Apple, MIT CCI) supply method and scale. The exploratory ones (voice, EEG aging) are your actual working field: real enough to build on, unfinished enough for genuine contributions — and exactly where narrative and evidence begin to drift apart.

The fringe and speculative entries stand here deliberately rather than being filtered out — you wanted the broad view. Their use is in demarcation: they mark what an attractive "the network feels more" story looks like when it lacks safeguarding. The further down an entry, the more indispensable the review instance from the contact list (QUEST) — not as a brake, but as what separates your project from the bottom line.

The theory band follows a different logic: there it is not about verifiability but about the concept itself. Malaspina and Hainge supply the philosophical version of what the whole atlas attempts in practice — and, via Simondon, Deleuze and Brassier, connect exactly to the line of thought that suits you.
