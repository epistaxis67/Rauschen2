From Conversation to the Unthinkable

A question set for the conversations with the experts from both contact cards. Built as a dramaturgy: from general interest in noise, through their own research field, to the reversal it all runs toward — the limit is not the technically feasible but imagination. The aim is not to interrogate the experts but to invite them to look, together, over the edge of the feasible.

Basic stance: each stage builds trust and shared vocabulary before the next asks more. Whoever starts at stage 5 ("what would you invent?") reaps defensiveness. Whoever works up from stage 1 has made the expert an accomplice of the question by then — and may, at the end, look over the edge of the feasible together. The why lines explain what each question does in the conversation.

01
Stage 1 · entry

The phenomenon — common ground

Goal: establish a shared understanding of "noise" before it turns technical. Shows seriousness and opens up.
1.1
What does "noise" mean in your daily work, concretely — an interference term, a measurement problem, or something more interesting?Opens neutrally and lets the expert define the term themselves.
1.2
Is there a famous story in your field where something was first taken for interference and later became a discovery?Activates the noise-becomes-signal figure with an example the expert chooses.
1.3
Where, for you, is the border between a real signal and mere chance — and how certain is that border actually?Introduces the central epistemological tension early, without pushing.
02
Stage 2 · their field

Their research interest — real listening

Goal: do justice to the person's specific interest. Here you listen, don't steer. This is the trust anchor.
2.1
What are you working on right now that really preoccupies you — and what about it is still unsolved?Pure invitation. You learn the living interest, not the textbook version.
2.2
What data accrues for you that you routinely discard or filter out because it's irrelevant to your actual question?The key question of the whole stage — leads gently to the side channel without valuing it yet.
2.3
If you had time and means, which aspect of your field would deserve more attention than it gets?Opens the space for underfunded, overlooked directions — often the overlooked lies there.
03
Stage 3 · the border

What counts as noise for you?

Goal: make the concrete border-drawing visible — which material falls on which side, and why exactly there?
3.1
By what criteria does your method decide what is treated as signal and what is subtracted as noise — and who set those criteria, when?Makes conscious that the border is a historical decision, not a law of nature (the Kittler point).
3.2
Are there patterns in your "noise" data you've already noticed but which have no explanation yet?Aims directly at anomaly-suspect material — the precursor of a nugget.
3.3
How much of what you filter out could you technically keep at all — and does anyone?Clarifies practical access to the raw material (the bottleneck from the devices card).
04
Stage 4 · the turn

When does noise become signal?

Goal: explore the conditions of the turn together — what would have to change for the discarded to become readable?
4.1
What would have to change — in measurement, compute, theory — for part of today's noise to become signal tomorrow?Invites the expert to imagine the turn themselves. They become co-author of the hypothesis.
4.2
If your discarded material were coupled with other data sources — which ones, and what might become visible?Introduces the aggregation/fusion idea, from the viewpoint of their field.
4.3
Where would you look for a spurious signal first — what would be the most obvious confusion to guard against?Brings in the safeguard and signals you work seriously, not just hopefully.
05
Stage 5 · think the unthinkable

Tracing the border of the imaginable

Goal: turn the question around. Not "where is a hidden signal," but what would be conceivable if the limit were imagination, not technology. The expert stays in their field — but is invited to look over its edge.
5.1
Suppose what you routinely filter out were not interference but carried information we merely lack the model for — what would one have to be able to imagine for it to become a signal?Turns the axis: not "is there a signal" but "what thinking would make one of it." Moves the limit from technology into imagination.
5.2
If you could invent a device that doesn't exist — one that reads out exactly what is filtered away today — what would it measure, and what could it make visible?Brings design fiction into the conversation as a tool. The conceived device lets you stay concrete and still think past the feasible.
5.3
Where, for you, is the border between a bold but serious idea and mere fantasy — what would you pin that on in the concrete case?Keeps the speculative flight grounded: lets the expert name the criterion for seriousness. Guards against arbitrariness without smothering imagination.
5.4
What about this whole figure of thought — that not the feasible but imagination is the real limit — do you consider overrated or naive?The most honest question. Invites objection, guards against self-deception, and paradoxically strengthens trust. Scientists especially take the invitation to object seriously.
// tailoring · accents by interlocutor
The set above fits everyone. These accents pick up the specific interest of individual profiles — one extra question each, slotted into stages 2–3. Illustrative, not exhaustive.
Theorist of neural noise · stochastic resonance
"Under what conditions does noise help signal transmission rather than disturbing it — and could that be induced deliberately?"
Fragmentomics / biomarker · data-trash pioneer
"Which discarded fraction of your material could, like the DNA fragments once, be the next source of information?"
Collective / swarm research · emergent sensing
"What transfers from your animal groups to human networks — and where does the analogy break?"
Neuro-measurement / EEG aging · aperiodic part
"How robust is the 1/f part really as a marker — and where does evidence end, where does narrative begin?"
Econometrics / aggregation · signal extraction
"How do you separate real signal from overfitting in high-dimensional, noisy data — and what is it worth economically?"
Metascience / review instance · corrective
"How do you fastest recognize that a celebrated 'signal' was in truth noise?"
Media / science theory · framing
"How has the term 'noise' shifted historically — and what does that reveal about today's border-drawing?"
Speculative fringes · with caution
"What distinguishes your approach methodically from a post-hoc pattern search in chance?" (probing, not ingratiating)

// how this set relates to the atlas

It is the link between the contact cards (who) and the practical work (Devices, Setups). The five stages retrace, in miniature, the path of the whole atlas: from the phenomenon (System) through the concrete (Devices/Setups) to the reversal the manifesto carries — from the usable to the imaginable. The tailoring lines pick up the profiles from both contact lists.

One principle carries the whole guide: you don't lift the discarded by asking experts for the hidden signal — but by drawing them so far into the question that they look, with you, over the edge of the feasible. For the limit is not technology but what we dare to conceive. And the most honest question (5.4) is the one that invites doubting exactly this figure of thought.
